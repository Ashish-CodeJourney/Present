    Turn-off guard

    Switching off takes a moment. A real person takes priority.

    Willpower is weakest at 11 PM. So Present adds a little friction exactly when you reach for the off switch, and offers a better option.

    • A countdown that doubles. 15 seconds the first time today, then 30, then 60.
    • A real-world social quest. Call someone you miss, make a chai plan, talk to someone nearby, step outside.
    • A gentler exit. "Pause for 15 minutes instead" turns itself back on.
    • Name it. To switch off completely, you write why. Naming it helps you notice it.

    🔒 Your privacy

    Your screen never leaves your phone.

    Recognising Reels happens in memory on your phone. What leaves it is the sentence you write for Gemini, and nothing else about your screen.

    👀 What Present looks at

    The screen's structure inside Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and Snapchat, so it can recognise Reels and Shorts. Other apps are ignored.

    🔒 What happens to it

    It's processed in memory on your phone: never stored, never sent. No screenshots, no OCR.

    ✉️ What leaves your phone

    The sentence you type to Gemini (plus your current rules when you edit them) and, for weekly insights, aggregate block counts. Blocking itself runs entirely on your phone and works offline.

    Present for Chrome

    The same promise on your laptop.

    A Manifest V3 extension hides YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels and Explore, and leaves search, subscriptions and DMs alone. Type your intent in the popup, compile it with Gemini, review, activate. The policy syncs with chrome.storage.sync.

    1. Build it from extension/ with npm run build.
    2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
    3. Load unpacked → pick extension/dist/. With no policy, Shorts and Reels are hidden by default.
